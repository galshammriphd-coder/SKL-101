      Foundations of artificial intelligence

      What AI is, why it matters, how systems differ, and how AI, machine learning and deep learning nest inside each other.

      Slides 10–19
      Artificial Intelligence refers to computer systems designed to perform functions associated with human intelligence, including perception, learning, reasoning, problem-solving, language interaction, and creative production.UNESCO, COMEST, Preliminary Study on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence, 2019; UNESCO, 2021
      PerceptionLearningReasoningProblem solvingLanguage interactionCreative production

      AI is difficult to define because it is a broad and evolving field. As computers advance, the range of tasks considered “intelligent” keeps expanding, so no single definition captures every system. AI is not one technology or one algorithm: it is an umbrella field.

      AI can be understood as systems that use technologies to collect and process data, generate predictions or recommendations, and support or make decisions with varying levels of autonomy in pursuit of specific goals.Saudi Data & Artificial Intelligence Authority (SDAIA)
      Collect data→Process & learn→Predict · recommend · decide→Take or support action→Achieve a defined goal

      AI is not simply about making machines “think like humans”. It is also about building systems that use data and computation to produce intelligent outputs and actions, with degrees of autonomy ranging from decision support to automated action.

      Why AI matters, and why capability ≠ hype

      What AI can contribute

      • Innovation & growth: new products, services and ways of working
      • Quality: more consistent, data-informed processes
      • Capabilities: extends human and organisational capacity
      • Efficiency: automates or optimises selected processes
      • Productivity: helps people accomplish tasks more effectively

      What rapid growth also creates

      • Widespread adoption
      • High expectations
      • Risk of exaggeration or misunderstanding
      • Unrealistic expectations about AI capabilities

      Source: SDAIA

      ✓

      Understanding what AI can do, cannot do, and under what conditions it works is essential for responsible adoption and sound decision-making.

      Seven criteria for distinguishing AI systems

      These are not separate categories: a single system usually combines several. An AI study assistant may use language, knowledge, reasoning, planning and tools; a computer-vision system may rely mainly on perception, learning and reasoning.

      4LearningCan it improve from data or experience?Machine learning, continuous improvement

      Currently deployed

      AGI · General

      i

      These categories describe breadth of intelligence, not technologies. A system can use deep learning, generative AI, tools or agents and still be narrow AI.

      AI ⊃ Machine learning ⊃ Deep learning

      • AI: the broad field of systems performing tasks associated with human intelligence. Example: a rule-based expert system.
      • ML: algorithms learn patterns from data rather than relying entirely on explicit rules. Example: predicting whether an image contains a condition.
      • DL: multi-layer neural networks learn complex representations from large data. Example: a deep network for medical image classification.
      !

      Deep learning does not reach conclusions “without human intervention”. It still needs human-defined objectives, data preparation, training, evaluation and validation.

      Machine learning vs deep learning

      ML:Human-designed features→Learning algorithm→Prediction
      DL:Raw data→Many network layers→Learned representations→Prediction / generation
      DimensionMachine learningDeep learning
      ApproachLearns patterns; often relies on human-selected features and structured dataMulti-layer networks learn features and representations automatically
      DataOften performs well with structured, moderate-sized datasetsOften benefits from large datasets, including images, audio and text
      Human involvementHumans select features, configure, validate and correctSubstantial human involvement in data, architecture, training and validation; feature extraction more automated
      HardwareMany models run efficiently on CPUsTraining large models often benefits from GPUs or accelerators
      Training timeSeconds to hoursHours to weeks or longer
      Typical applicationsClassification, regression, forecasting, recommendation, fraud detectionComputer vision, speech, NLP, autonomous systems, generative AI
      Is it true that deep learning always requires GPUs?
      No. Deep learning can run on CPUs; GPUs or other accelerators are often preferred for computationally intensive training. Likewise, “ML = simple, DL = complex” is too simplistic: the choice depends on the data, problem, resources and requirements.

      Generative AI and large language models

      How GenAI differs from traditional AI, what it offers healthcare in the KSA, and what an LLM is (and is not).

      Slides 20–28
      Generative AI is a class of AI systems that can generate new content by learning patterns from data and using those patterns to produce outputs in response to user instructions.
      Learn patterns→Interpret the instruction→Generate new content
      TextImagesAudioVideoCodeOther digital content

      Unlike systems built only to classify, predict or retrieve, GenAI produces new outputs, not copies of a single training example. In healthcare education it can produce study summaries, explanations, practice questions, diagrams and draft content; outputs must be reviewed and verified.

      Traditional AI vs generative AI

      Traditional AI

      Input→Analyse→Predict / classify→Output

      Asks: “What is this?” “What is likely to happen?”

      Goal: predict, classify, detect, or decide. Data: often structured and/or labelled. Output: a label, score, prediction or decision.

      Example: “Is this medical image likely to show pneumonia?”

      Generative AI

      Prompt→Understand patterns→Generate / transform→New content

      Asks: “Create something based on what I asked.”

      Goal: generate new content. Data: large-scale, often self-supervised. Output: text, image, audio, video, code.

      Example: “Summarise this medical article for a first-year medical student.”

      i

      The distinction is functional, not absolute. Modern systems can combine predictive, retrieval, reasoning and generative capabilities.

      Five benefits of GenAI for healthcare transformation in the KSA

      Increased efficiency & productivity

      • Summarising lengthy documents and educational materials
      • Transcribing spoken or recorded content
      • Drafting reports, correspondence and educational resources
      • Generating images, diagrams and other digital content

      Potential benefit: less time on repetitive tasks, more time for higher-value human work.

      Improved communication & collaboration

      • Content creation for education and communication
      • Conversational interfaces and virtual assistants
      • Multilingual communication and content adaptation
      • Collaborative knowledge environments
      • AI-assisted information exchange across teams

      Potential benefit: better access to information, more effective human–human and human–AI collaboration.

      Informed decision-making

      Understand→Compare→Explore→Decide

      GenAI organises, summarises and interprets text, audio, images, video and structured or unstructured data; it helps explore scenarios and compare alternatives. Healthcare examples: summarising literature, organising educational resources, comparing documented scenarios, supporting administrative planning.

      ✓

      AI supports the decision; humans remain responsible for the decision.

      Enhanced accessibility & inclusion

      Adapt→Translate→Simplify→Include
      • Adapting content to different learning and accessibility needs
      • Translating and simplifying complex information
      • Supporting multilingual communication
      • Generating content adapted to local language and cultural context
      • Creating alternative formats: text, audio, visual, simplified

      Elevated public service quality

      • Personalising interactions based on user needs and context
      • Conversational access to information and services
      • Clear, simplified and multilingual content
      • 24/7 self-service for routine inquiries
      • Helping employees find, summarise and respond to information
      • More consistent and accessible digital services

      Applications in the KSA: healthcare (patient-facing information and navigation), education (personalised learning), social services (service guidance), legal services (document assistance).

      !

      AI-assisted ≠ AI-autonomous. Human oversight, verification, privacy and governance remain essential.

      What are large language models?

      LLMs are large-scale AI models trained on extensive collections of text and other language data to learn patterns in human language and generate coherent responses.

      How it works

      Language data→Large-scale training→Learned patterns
      User prompt→LLM→Generated response

      Examples: GPT, Gemini, LLaMA. Tasks: answer, summarise, translate, write, code, transform.

      LLM ≠ search engine

      An LLM generates responses from learned patterns and whatever is available through its context and connected tools. It does not automatically have internet access or up-to-date information unless a tool or retrieval system is provided.

      Applications of LLMs in medicine

      1 · Educate

      Explain concepts; practice questions and clinical scenarios; exam prep; simulated patient–clinician conversations.

      2 · Inform

      Summarise papers and guidelines; extract key findings; answer from provided or trusted sources; organise large volumes of information.

      3 · Document

      Draft and summarise clinical notes; structure unstructured text; support coding workflows; assist report drafting.

      4 · Communicate

      Translate health content; patient-friendly explanations; multilingual communication; clarity and readability.

      5 · Support decisions

      Organise patient information; explore considerations; differential-diagnosis reasoning. Supports, but does not replace, clinical judgment.

      6 · Augment workflows

      Generate and transform content; information extraction; administrative communication; generate or explain code.

      Generate ≠ Verify  |  Assist ≠ Decide  |  Human oversight remains essential

      Prompt engineering

      Designing better instructions, improving them one requirement at a time, and remembering that a better prompt is not a guaranteed truth.

      Slides 29–48 · includes Activity A1 and the model-comparison tutorial
      Prompt engineering is the systematic process of designing, structuring, and refining instructions to guide a Generative AI system toward a desired output.

      Why it matters

      • Reduces ambiguity and vague responses
      • Improves relevance and structure of outputs
      • Makes responses easier to evaluate and verify
      • Adapts outputs to an audience, task or purpose
      • Supports more consistent human–AI interaction

      Applications in healthcare

      Medical education: explanations, summaries, practice questions.
      Research: literature organisation, information extraction.
      Documentation: drafting and restructuring text.
      Communication: adapting information for different audiences.

      ✓

      Prompt engineering is not about asking AI more questions. It is about designing better instructions and systematically evaluating the results.

      Six common prompting techniques

      Instruction only. No examples are provided; the model responds from the instruction and its learned capabilities.

      “List the major risk factors for stroke.”

      Best for straightforward questions and well-defined tasks.

      Instruction + examples. A few examples demonstrate the expected pattern, style or format, so the model can infer what is expected.

      Q: What is diabetes? A: A condition characterised by elevated blood glucose. Q: What is hypertension? A: ?

      Best for classification, formatting, style adaptation and consistent responses.

      Instruction + criteria or steps. Ask for a concise, verifiable explanation or explicit solution steps, not the model’s private internal reasoning.

      “For this educational case, identify the relevant findings, list the clinical criteria that should be considered, and explain the conclusion briefly.”

      Best for complex educational problems. The example is deliberately an educational case, not a live clinical decision.

      Role sets the perspective: expertise level, audience and communication style.

      “Act as a medical ethics educator. Explain to first-year medical students why patient confidentiality is essential in clinical practice.”
      !

      A role does not make the AI a qualified professional.

      Instruction defines the task: clear, specific directions about what to do and how to present it (length, format, scope, style).

      “Explain the difference between Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes in no more than 100 words. Use a simple comparison and highlight two key differences.”

      Context provides the situation: relevant background so the AI can respond appropriately to the task and audience.

      “A 65-year-old patient with diabetes is being discussed in a classroom case. Explain the major categories of information a clinician would consider when evaluating chest pain. Do not provide a diagnosis or treatment recommendation.”
      Role + Instruction + Context → more relevant, better-structured output → verify

      What a good prompt carries

      ContextTaskConstraintsAudienceFormatExamplesDesired outcome

      Weak prompt

      “Explain diabetes.”

      Too open: the AI does not know for whom, why, how much, or in what format.

      Better prompt

      “I am a first-year medical student revising for an MCQ. Explain Type 1 vs Type 2 diabetes in under 150 words as a two-column table, with one exam-style question at the end.”

      Who: first-year student · Why: MCQ revision · What: Type 1 vs Type 2 · How much: ≤150 words · Format: table + question

      ✓

      Don’t just ask AI what you want. Tell it what a useful answer looks like. The goal is not a longer prompt; it is the right information.

      What prompt quality improves

      • Relevance: the answer stays tied to what you asked
      • Usefulness: the output is more useful to you
      • Checkability: easier to evaluate and verify

      But it does not automatically improve the model’s underlying knowledge.

      Fluent ≠ Accurate · Structured ≠ Verified — Prompting improves the question. Verification protects the answer.

      A professional prompt-writing structure

      #ElementQuestionCovers

      Healthcare analogy

      A clinician understands blood pressure but needs a cuff to measure it. An AI model may explain a calculation; a calculator tool performs it.

      Understands→Uses instrument→Gets measurement

      “What is the current WHO recommendation?”
      Model alone: may answer from learned knowledge.
      Model + search tool: can retrieve current information from an external source.

      If ChatGPT gives you today’s weather, did the model go outside and measure it?
      No. The system used a search or weather tool connected to the model. The model provides the intelligence; the tool provides the external capability. Current information requires an appropriate external source or tool.

      Skills and the Challenger

      From giving AI instructions once to giving it a reusable capability, and using a Skill to test claims instead of confirming them.

      Slides 84–94 · Activity A2 and the Claude Skill exercise
      A Skill is a reusable capability that tells an AI system how to perform a particular type of task consistently.

      The recipe · Skill

      Instructions + rules + criteria. Defines how the task should be performed.

      The cook · Model

      Interprets and applies the Skill; generates and processes information.

      The oven · Tool

      Provides an external capability or action when needed.

      Skill = how to do the task  ·  Tool = what the AI can use to do it

      Implementation differs across products: a skill file, custom instructions, a configured GPT, a Gem or similar reusable configuration. The idea is the same: package instructions, rules, examples and resources for a repeatable task.

      Anatomy of a Skill: the eleven-field template

      A prompt asks AI to perform a task once. A Skill defines a reusable method for performing it consistently. Fill the template below; the specification is assembled for you to copy into Claude, a GPT, or a Gem.

      Start from:

      Memory line: identity → input → method → limits → output → quality → failure → example.

      What makes a Skill good?

      • One clear task (focused): one Skill, one main purpose
      • Instructions anyone could follow (explicit): not dependent on the creator’s assumptions
      • A clear output shape (structured): e.g. Summary → Key concepts → 3 review questions
      • A failure condition (bounded): “If the input is not lecture notes, stop and state that it is outside the Skill’s scope.”
      • An example (demonstrated): show the expected behaviour

      Spot the problem

      “Help students learn biology and summarise lectures and make questions and explain difficult topics.”

      What is wrong with it?
      It bundles four unrelated tasks, gives no instructions, no output shape, no stopping condition and no example. Fix it by asking: What is the one task? What instructions are missing? What should the output look like? When should it stop? What example would clarify it?
      Good Skill = Focused + Explicit + Structured + Bounded + Demonstrated
      Activity A2 · Build your first AI SkillPairs · 20 minutes · healthcare education only
      1. Design: complete all 11 fields of the template.
      2. Test: run your Skill on one realistic input.
      3. Inspect: examine the output carefully.
      4. Identify one failure: missing info, unclear output, wrong format, ignored constraint, or wrong level.
      5. Fix: modify the relevant field(s), run again, record the improvement.

      Swap: Pair A gives its Skill to Pair B, who test it as users (without rewriting) and report what worked, what failed and what should change. No diagnosis and no patient-specific clinical decisions.

      The Challenger Skill: stop AI from simply agreeing

      Assistants are tuned to be helpful and cooperative. A Challenger Skill asks AI to test a claim instead of confirming it: not “Why am I right?” but “What would make me wrong?”. Step through the nine moves.

      !

      The Challenger is a critical-thinking tool, not a truth detector. Its output still needs verification.

      Challenger in action: “AI will replace doctors”

      Instead of asking “Is this true?”, the Challenger decomposes the claim into five questions.

      Which assumptions?

      What must be true for the claim to hold?

      Which tasks?

      Which parts of medical work are actually being discussed?

      What evidence?

      What supports or contradicts the claim?

      What limitations?

      Technical, human, organisational or regulatory constraints.

      What alternatives?

      What other futures or interpretations are possible?

      Pair challenge

      Challenge one belief: “AI always saves time”, “More data always produces better AI”, “AI is objective”, “AI will make learning easier”, “Confident-sounding AI is reliable”.

      Claim→Assumptions→Challenges→Evidence needed→Alternative scenarios→What would change my mind?

      Challenger agents

      A Challenger Skill can be assigned to a second agent that reviews and stress-tests the first. Example: the main agent creates an exam study plan; the Challenger asks “What important topics, learning outcomes or weak areas are missing?”; the main agent revises.

      Plan→Challenge→Revise→Recheck ↺
      i

      The Challenger is not another source of truth. It is a second layer of scrutiny.

      Exercise · Build a healthcare education Skill in ClaudePairs · 10 minutes

      Scenario: Claude as a Medical Education Summarizer for first-year students, turning lecture notes into a concise study guide. Include role, input, task, constraints, output and quality check. Output must be: key concepts → simple explanation → important terms → 3 review questions.

      Suggested input: “Inflammation is a protective response to tissue injury or infection. Acute inflammation involves vascular changes and recruitment of leukocytes to the affected tissue…”

      Reveal the model solution

      Role: Act as a medical education assistant for first-year medical students.
      Input: Lecture notes or educational text provided by the student.
      Task: Transform the material into a concise, accurate study guide, preserving essential concepts and terminology.
      Constraints: Clear first-year language · no information unsupported by the provided text · preserve scientific terminology · keep it concise · no diagnosis or treatment recommendations.
      Output: 1 Key concepts (3–5) · 2 Simple explanation · 3 Important terms (term / meaning) · 4 Three review questions that test understanding, not memorisation.
      Quality check: Are key concepts supported by the source? Has any meaning changed? Are explanations right for first-year students? Are the questions answerable from the material?

      Discuss: what would you change to make it more useful? (e.g. word limits per section, page references to the notes, a “common misconceptions” section, adaptation for Arabic-speaking students.)

      The problem MCP solves

      MCP = Model Context Protocol: an open protocol designed to standardise how AI applications connect with external tools, resources and contextual information.

      Without a standard

      More tools → more custom wiring → more complexity.

      With MCP

      One standard → many possible connections. Think of a universal connection standard (an analogy, not a literal equivalence).

      i

      MCP is not the tool. It is the standardised communication layer between an AI application and its tools.

      Host, client, server

      MCP roleWhat it isMental model
      HostThe AI application the user interacts withThe workplace
      ClientThe component inside the Host that communicates using MCPThe connector
      ServerA local or remote program that exposes Tools, Resources and PromptsThe provider
      User→Host ⟨Client⟩⇄ MCP ⇄MCP Server→Files · Database · Calendar · Search
      !

      Before you click “Connect”, know which tools and resources you are granting access to. A “server” is not necessarily a remote cloud server.

      From a manual task to a controlled sequence of automated steps, and knowing when not to automate.

      The automation pattern

      Every stage can fail. For higher-impact actions, the human check must be explicit. Put the eight stages in the right order by clicking them in sequence.

        StageQuestionExample: student submissions
        1 TriggerWhat starts the workflow?New form submitted
        2 InputWhat information enters?Student submission

        !

        No unchecked AI output should automatically trigger a consequential action. Design the check before the action, not after it.

        Is an automated workflow necessarily an agent?
        No. An automated workflow follows a predefined sequence and does not become an agent simply because AI is one of its steps. An agent is given a goal and determines which steps to take, adapting to intermediate results.

        The AI automation cycle in healthcare

        01 Input→02 Process→03 Decision support→04 Human check→05 Action→06 Monitor & improve ↺

        Patient support

        Answer routine questions; assist appointment workflows.

        Medical imaging

        Flag potentially abnormal findings for professional review.

        Clinical monitoring

        Generate early-warning alerts from patient data.

        Administration

        Support coding, billing, documentation and scheduling.

        Many AI systems do not learn automatically from use; updates need retraining, evaluation, approval and revalidation. Automation does not remove human responsibility.

        When not to automate

        The task is rare

        Little repetition, little value: cost and complexity may outweigh the benefit.

        The thinking is the learning

        If you must develop the reasoning yourself, automating it undermines the objective.

        Sensitive data is involved

        Keep restricted information out unless system, permissions and rules explicitly allow it.

        No effective check

        If you cannot explain how a wrong output would be caught before harm, it is not ready.

        !

        “One bad prompt can become one hundred bad summaries.” Automation reproduces the same error consistently and at scale.

        Local-first: cloud AI vs local AI

        The key difference is where the AI processing takes place. Local-first is not “cloud is bad”; it is a data-and-infrastructure decision.

        Cloud AI

        • Processing on remote infrastructure
        • Needs network connectivity
        • Data may leave the local environment
        • Access to powerful models
        • Provider manages much of the infrastructure
        • Institution sets rules for approved use

        Local AI

        • Processing on local infrastructure
        • Can work offline, depending on setup
        • Data can stay in the local environment
        • Performance depends on local hardware
        • User or institution maintains models and updates
        • Still needs security and governance

        Decide by data sensitivity, required capability, connectivity, cost, governance and hardware. Local AI is not automatically more private: that depends on how it is configured, secured and governed.

        AI agents

        From following steps to pursuing a goal: the agent loop, who decides what happens next, and where the human stays.

        Slides 113–125 · Activity A7 agent lab and tutorials
        An AI agent is a goal-oriented AI system that can pursue a goal through multiple steps, use tools, observe results, and determine subsequent actions until a defined stopping condition is reached.
        Goal→Plan→Act→Observe→Decide→Act …→Stop

        A workflow says

        “Do these steps in this order.”

        An agent says

        “Achieve this goal, using these capabilities and constraints.”

        !

        No stopping condition = uncontrolled execution. Examples: goal achieved, maximum steps reached, required evidence unavailable, human approval required, error or safety condition triggered.

        Three kinds of chatbot

        01 Rule-based

        Predefined rules, menus, keywords. Specific, predictable tasks.

        “Track my appointment.”

        02 AI conversational

        Understands natural language and uses context; may combine NLP, ML, retrieval and tools.

        “Help me find information about my appointment.”

        03 Generative AI

        LLM-powered: generates new responses; summarises, explains, transforms; may use files, search or code.

        “Explain this lecture and generate five practice questions.”

        Not every chatbot is generative AI, but generative AI can power highly capable conversational assistants.

        Chatbot vs workflow vs agent: who decides what happens next?

        ChatbotWorkflowAgent
        Who chooses the next step?The userThe designerThe system, within limits
        PatternQuestion → answer → next questionTrigger → step 1 → 2 → 3 → outputGoal → plan → act → observe → adapt → complete
        CharacterUsually reactiveUsually predictableGoal-oriented and adaptive
        Need for constraints & oversightLowerMediumHigher
        What makes an agent different from a chatbot?
        A chatbot primarily responds to user inputs; an agent can pursue a goal across multiple steps and determine or adapt its next action based on observations and results. “Agent” is not just a product label: autonomy can be bounded by tools, permissions, rules, step limits and human approval.

        Agent architecture: a walk-through

        Goal: “Prepare me for the renal exam.” Step through one complete loop.

        Skill · how

        Tells the agent how to perform a task.

        Tool · act

        Gives the agent the ability to act.

        API / MCP · connect

        Connects the agent to external systems.

        Memory · state

        Keeps context and progress across steps.

        Where does the human stay in the loop?

        Human-in-the-loop (HITL)

        Goal→Agent→Human approval→Action

        Human approves before action. The agent stops and waits.

        Human-on-the-loop (HOTL)

        Goal→Agent→Actions→Human monitoring

        Human monitors during execution and can stop or override.

        Classify the control level

        Pick the right control for each action. Practical rule: if an agent can send, delete, pay, publish, or access restricted data, place a human checkpoint before the action.

        Vibe coding

        Building software by describing it, then testing whether it actually works.

        Slides 126–136 · live example, study-planner lab, four tools
        Vibe coding is an AI-assisted approach to software development in which people use natural language and AI tools to create, modify, debug, and improve software iteratively.Term popularised by Andrej Karpathy (Feb 2025); Collins Dictionary Word of the Year 2025

        AI-assisted programming

        The developer reads and edits the code.

        Vibe coding

        The user judges the resulting behaviour and directs AI iteratively.

        !

        Vibe coding ≠ “programming is no longer needed”. It lowers the barrier to building software, not the responsibility to understand, test and validate it: requirements, testing, validation, security, correctness.

        The workflow is CTCO applied to software

        PromptingVibe coding
        ContextProject, user, environment
        TaskSoftware requirement
        ConstraintsTechnical and functional limits
        OutputExpected behaviour or interface
        VerifyRun and test the software
        Idea→Describe→AI generates prototype→Run→Test→Identify problems→Ask AI to fix→Retest→Improve→Document

        Generated code can look correct while producing incorrect behaviour. Test the behaviour, not whether AI produced code.

        Use AI as a learning and productivity aid while remaining responsible for how it is used and what it produces.

        4 Privacy & confidentiality

        Do not upload patient-identifiable, confidential or restricted information into unauthorised tools.

        Check important AI-generated information against reliable sources.

        Accuracy, hallucination and verification

        AI content can contain

        • Incorrect facts
        • Fabricated references
        • Outdated information
        • Missing context
        • Misleading explanations
        • Overconfident conclusions

        The verification loop

        GenerateInspectCheck the sourceCompareCorrectUse

        For medical claims, check

        • Textbooks
        • Peer-reviewed research
        • Official guidelines
        • Trusted medical databases
        • Instructor-provided materials
        i

        AI output is a starting point for evaluation, not automatically verified knowledge.

        Bias, fairness and responsible use

        AI outputs may contain

        Representation bias: some populations under-represented in training data.
        Language & cultural bias: responses differ across languages and contexts.
        Stereotyping: assumptions about groups or individuals.
        Accessibility gaps: content may not work equally well for all learners.

        What students should do

        Question: is the output making an assumption?
        Check: is there evidence?
        Compare: would it change for another population or context?
        Correct: remove unsupported or inappropriate claims.
        Disclose: explain AI involvement when required.
